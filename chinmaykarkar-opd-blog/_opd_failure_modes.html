Failure Modes of On-Policy DistillationDetailed visual companion — each mode shown individually

1 The Token-Level KL Is a Fragile Proxy

The per-token sampled KL is biased relative to the sequence-level KL the theory wants. The bias worsens with future-reward coupling, and gradient variance inflates on long trajectories.
Left: the token-level KL diverges from the sequence-level target as position increases, with growing bias and variance. Right: gradient variance explodes for long sequences, destabilising training exactly where OPD is most needed.

2 Prefix Drift & The Unreliable Teacher

Once the student makes a mistake, subsequent tokens leave the teacher's support. The teacher's signal degrades from informative → noisy → harmful along the drifted prefix.
The student's error at y₄ pushes the trajectory out of the teacher's support. The teacher's distribution goes from peaked and informative (left) to flat/random (right). The student earnestly distils this noise as if it were signal.

3 Local Teachability Collapse

Even when the teacher stays calibrated, its margin shrinks along the trajectory. The KL gradient carries less and less signal — not because it's wrong, but because it's barely informative.
The teacher's margin (how much it "knows" at each position) decays along the trajectory. Past the change-point, the gradient carries almost no information but still contributes compute cost and noise.

4 Rock Tokens & Imbalanced Supervision

~18% of tokens exhibit persistently high loss that never decreases, absorbing disproportionate gradient bandwidth. Meanwhile most tokens contribute near-zero gradient.
Left: most tokens cluster near zero loss, but a persistent ~18% "rock token" tail absorbs disproportionate gradient norm. Right: rock-token loss never decreases over training — the student cannot and should not learn them, yet they consume ~40% of optimisation bandwidth.

5 Diversity Collapse: The Precision–Recall Trade-off

Aggressive reverse-KL OPD concentrates mass on one strategy per prompt. Pass@1 goes up, Pass@k goes down. The student loses the ability to explore alternative solutions.
Reverse-KL OPD seeks the best single mode and ignores the rest. Greedy decoding improves (Pass@1 ↑), but sampling diversity collapses (Pass@k ↓). The student loses the ability to find alternative solutions when the primary one fails.

6 The Privileged Information Gap & Calibration

The PI-conditioned teacher's confidence is computed under information the deployed student cannot see. Distilling that confidence produces a miscalibrated student that is overconfident on hard problems.
The teacher's confidence P(correct|x, PI) is not a valid target for the student's deployment confidence P(correct|x). The student inherits overconfidence, worst on the hardest problems where PI provides the biggest lift. Hedging language gets compressed away, amplifying the effect.

7 Self-Play Saturation

When both views share weights, they can converge on a confident-but-wrong heuristic. The gradient collapses to zero, and the model self-reinforces the flawed trajectory — structurally similar to GAN mode collapse.
When teacher and student share weights, a confident-but-wrong reasoning heuristic makes both views agree. The log-ratio signal vanishes, the gradient drops to zero, and the model self-reinforces the error with no mechanism to escape.

8 Teacher Capability Is Doing More Work Than Admitted

Every method assumes the teacher (given PI) is meaningfully better. When the gap is small, dense supervision becomes dense noise. The pipeline degenerates quietly.
Left: when the teacher has a clear advantage from PI, distillation gives strong signal. Right: when the gap is marginal, the same dense supervision becomes dense noise. The pipeline works best with shared-rule PI and degrades with instance-specific PI on tasks where the teacher is also weak.
