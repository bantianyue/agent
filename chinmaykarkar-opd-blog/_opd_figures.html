Figure 1 — Mode Covering vs Mode Seeking

Forward KL · Mode Covering
Reverse KL · Mode Seeking
Left: Forward KL forces the student to spread mass across all teacher modes, wasting capacity. Right: Reverse KL lets the student concentrate on the mode it can best represent and ignore the rest.

Figure 2 — Error Compounding: Off-Policy O(εT²) vs On-Policy O(εT)

Off-policy training accumulates errors quadratically because each mistake pushes the model further out-of-distribution. On-policy training keeps errors linear by training on the model's own prefixes.

Figure 3 — Prefix Drift: When the Teacher Loses Its Way

Once the student makes an error at y₄, subsequent tokens drift out of the teacher's training distribution. The teacher's signal quality degrades along the trajectory, and the student distils increasingly unreliable feedback.

Figure 4 — The On-Policy Distillation Loop

The on-policy loop: the student generates its own data, the teacher scores it, the loss computes divergence, and the updated student generates the next batch. Training distribution always matches inference distribution.

Figure 5 — On-Policy Self-Distillation: Two Views, Same Weights

Self-distillation: both views share the same weights. The PI-conditioned view is sharper (it knows the answer), and the per-token log-ratio between views becomes the learning signal. No external teacher needed.
