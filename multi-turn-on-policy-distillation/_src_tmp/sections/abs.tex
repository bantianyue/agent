We study on-policy distillation (OPD) for agentic tasks, where an LLM agent interacts with an environment over multiple turns and a student imitates a teacher over these multi-turn interaction histories. Fully online OPD is costly because each update requires fresh student rollouts through the environment and teacher queries at visited histories. We propose Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD), an off-environment alternative that reuses pre-collected teacher trajectories as replayed prefixes: the student acts at selected steps, while the teacher provides dense per-step supervision without executing new environment interactions. We show that multi-turn OPD introduces a prefix trap: making histories more student-on-policy improves relevance to the student, but can query the teacher on histories where its target is unreliable. This creates a two-sided distribution shift between student occupancy and teacher reliability. ReOPD addresses this by treating multi-turn OPD as a reliability-aware prefix distribution design and implements it with a simple step-decaying sampling schedule that emphasizes early, lower-shift prefixes. Across mathematical reasoning with Python and search environments over multiple teacher and student model scales, ReOPD preserves or improves OPD-level accuracy, uses zero tool calls during student training, and is at least 4$\times$ faster per rollout than OPD. ReOPD therefore turns expensive agent-environment interaction into a reusable offline resource, enabling scalable distillation across tools, tasks, and environments.

% We study Replayed-Prefix On-Policy Distillation (OPD), where a student imitates a teacher on
% multi-step agentic histories that should stay relevant to the student's own
% behavior. With fully online rollouts this is costly: each update rolls the student
% through the environment and queries the teacher afresh at every visited step. We
% examine an efficient alternative, \emph{prefix replay}, that reuses a fixed pool of
% pre-collected teacher trajectories: the student is rolled in on a teacher-recorded prefix and produces its
% own action at that step, the teacher supplies the per-step target, and the prefix and
% its observations are replayed from the trace so the environment is never queried.
% This removes online interaction but not the temporal structure of agentic learning:
% prefixes act as implicit states, and multi-step generation induces a trajectory
% distribution over them.

% We identify a prefix trap with two layers. Temporally, prefix errors compound across
% steps, so the problem stays sequential rather than ordinary supervised distillation.
% Distributionally, it induces a two-sided shift: making prefixes fully
% student-on-policy keeps them relevant to the student (countering a student occupancy
% shift) but can query the teacher where its per-step target is no longer trustworthy
% (a teacher reliability shift). Agentic OPD is therefore not merely making
% distillation on-policy but reliability-aware prefix distribution design, balancing
% student relevance against teacher reliability. We instantiate this as Agentic
% On-Policy Distillation (ReOPD), which realizes the effective prefix distribution
% off-environment with a step-decaying sampling schedule: it leans on teacher-supported
% prefixes when they are the safer supervision region and becomes OPD-like when
% student-induced prefixes are safe. Across several
% environments and teachers of varying scale, ReOPD matches or surpasses
% student-on-policy OPD while removing online environment interaction, reaching strong
% students at a fraction of the rollout cost.
