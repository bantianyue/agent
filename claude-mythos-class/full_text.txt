Methodology: We use formulas and approaches used in estimating training costs for DeepSeek V3, ARCEE, KIMI 2.5 etc. We will use GPU performance numbers from Nvidia Data-sheets for B200/B300 and NVL72 racks, and statistics on data availability from Epoch AI. When we say B200/B300 we are actually referring them in GB200/GB300 superchips, as a part of NVL72 racks. NVL72 has world size of 72GPUs, it makes it easier to train models in this class as those 72 GPUs can access each others HBM at high throughputs through NVLink/NVSwitch connectivity. That said data parallelism alone is not enough. You still need other parallelism (pipeline, tensor, sequence). To account for all the bottlenecks, we assume MFU (Model Flop Utilisation) of only 20%.

2. Training Data Requirements

Formula:

D = tokens_per_parameter × N_total

D = 40 × N_total

Token count is based on total parameters — all expert weights must be trained across the full run, even though only 5% activate per token.

The publicly crawlable internet provides ~100T tokens (Epoch AI analysis. I have highlighted 100T tokens mark below). Models requiring more must rely on synthetic data generation, rephrasing, or multi-epoch training. As we will see later, synthetic data generation becomes very costly as we approach 10T parameter class.

3. Sparsity Ratios of Frontier MoE Models

To justify the 5% active ratio, we surveyed the latest frontier models:

Why 5% Is a Sound Assumption

· DeepSeek V3/V3.2 (5.4%), GLM-5 (5.4%), and MiniMax M2.5 (4.3%) all cluster around 4–6%. 5% is the center of this range.

· Kimi K2.5 already achieves 3.1% — the trend is toward more sparsity. 5% is conservative for a next-gen model.

· Older models (Mixtral, DBRX) used ~27% active. In 18 months the field moved to 3–6%, a decisive shift.

· DeepSeek V3 trained successfully at 5.5% active on 14.8T tokens with no quality loss vs. dense baselines.

· 5% active yields practical sizes: 2T→100B, 4T→200B, 10T→500B active per token — all proven architecture scales.

4. Training Compute (FLOPs)

With MoE, only the active parameters compute per token:

N_active = N_total × 0.05

C = 6 × N_active × D

C = 6 × (N_total × 0.05) × (40 × N_total)

C = 12 × N_total²

5. GPU Specifications

B300 only outperforms B200 at FP4. At BF16, they are identical (2.25 PFLOPS).

6. Calculation Chain

Step 1: Effective_FLOPS = Peak_FLOPS × 0.20

Step 2: GPU_seconds = C ÷ Effective_FLOPS

Step 3: GPU_hours = GPU_seconds ÷ 3,600

Step 4: GPUs_needed = GPU_hours ÷ 2,160 (= 90 days × 24 hrs)

Step 5: NVL72_Racks = GPUs_needed ÷ 72

Step 6: Total_Cost = GPU_hours × $/GPU/hr

7. FP4 Scenario

Theoretical best case — all FLOPs at FP4 throughput.

From what I know it is not possible today, or not proven today at this scale. Nvidia has done it for smaller models like 12B with 4 bit precision training. But, even there they maintained many other things in higher precision. To quote: “Attention, embedding, non-linear layers, and other tensors: To ensure numerical stability during training, we retain the original precision (e.g., BF16 or FP32) for embeddings, the output projection head, normalization layers, non-linearities, and attention components, including softmax and the query-key and attention score-value batched GEMMs. The main weights (stored by the optimizer), weight gradients (used for gradient accumulation across microbatches and across data-parallel replicas), and optimizer states are also kept in FP32. Tensor parallel reductions are performed in BF16 precision.”

B200 (FP4) — $5/GPU/hr
B300 (FP4) — $6/GPU/hr

11. Synthetic Data Generation Cost

The 10T model requires 400T tokens but only ~100T are available from the internet. The remaining 300T must be generated synthetically. We analyze the cost using Claude Sonnet 4.6 as a representative frontier API model.

Sonnet 4.6 Pricing
Cost to Generate 300T Synthetic Tokens

Assuming a 1:10 input-to-output ratio (30T prompt tokens → 300T generated tokens):

Standard: (30T ÷ 1M × $3) + (300T ÷ 1M × $15) = $90M + $4,500M = $4.6B

Batch: (30T ÷ 1M × $1.50) + (300T ÷ 1M × $7.50) = $45M + $2,250M = $2.3B

12. Total Cost: Training + Synthetic Data (10T class)

Figure 3: 10T class Cost Breakdown — Training vs Synthetic Data

Practical alternatives to save on synthetic data costs

· Self-hosted inference: Running a Llama-class (MiniMax etc would be good - as they have fewer active parameters and robust performance) model on owned GPUs costs ~$50-100M for 300T tokens — 20-40× cheaper than Sonnet 4.6 API.

· Multi-epoch training: Train on the same 100T tokens 4× with curriculum learning. Zero data generation cost, though diminishing returns after 2-3 epochs.

· Rephrasing/augmentation: Take 100T internet tokens and rephrase 3-4× using a cheap model. Much cheaper than generating from scratch.

· Hybrid approach: 100T internet + 50T high-quality synthetic + 3 epochs approximately 400T effective tokens at minimal generation cost.

13. What about post training?

We mostly focus on pre-training and synthetic data generation cost. Post training (SFT + RL) can cost additional 20% (Epoch AI’s analysis of DeepSeek R1). xAI have reported spent almost as much compute on post training for Grok 4 as on pre-training of the model.

Key Findings of this thought exercise

2T class is very achievable: 191 NVL72 racks, $148M at BF16. NeoLabs can still manage it. Data requirement (80T) is within internet availability.

4T class is feasible: 762 racks, $593M at BF16. Needs ~60T synthetic tokens to reach total of 160T tokens — manageable but starts getting very expensive.

10T class is within reach with hyper-scaler partners and truck load of cash: 4,763 racks, $3.7B at BF16. But 300T synthetic tokens add $2.3B (API) or ~$75M (self-hosted cheaper models).

Synthetic data can cost as much as training: At API pricing, 300T tokens via Sonnet 4.6 costs $2.3B — it is almost 60% of $3.7B training cost for 10T class model on B200 with BF16 precision. Self-hosted generation may become essential at this scale.

B300 only wins at FP4: At BF16, identical throughput to B200 but 20% more expensive. As I understand, Nvidia positions B300 for inference, rather than training.

Data is a major bottleneck: Not compute or GPUs alone— the 400T token requirement for 10T models is probably the hardest constraint to satisfy!!!!
